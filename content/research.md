My research focuses on **nanopore single-molecule detection devices and biomedical analytical methods**, at the crossroads of engineering and medicine. The central theme is to turn nanopores from passive sensors into **actively controllable, repeatedly readable single-molecule analysis platforms**.

## Research Directions

### 1. Nanopore molecular recognition for protein modification and drug analysis

Based on semi-rationally designed aerolysin nanopores, we developed a single-molecule discrimination method for protein **citrullination**, achieving molecular distinction at the **sub-dalton** mass-difference level — providing a new analytical tool for citrullination research and related drug development. Engineered nanopores are further applied to neutral glycans, sialoglycans, and **cisplatin–DNA adduct** detection, serving glycan-related biomarker analysis and drug–nucleic acid interaction studies.

### 2. Nanopore device development and single-molecule manipulation platforms

We develop core solid-state nanopore detection modules and build single-molecule detection and motion-control platforms. The **molecular ping-pong / repeated recapture technology** enables multiple measurements of the *same* molecule, reducing the random fluctuation of single translocation events and improving the consistency of molecular characterization — providing device and instrument support for biomedical and pharmaceutical analysis.

### 3. Mechanism and theory-guided detection optimization

Combining **molecular dynamics simulation** with multiscale theoretical calculations, we study how molecular conformation, pore-interface interactions, and trans-pore transport shape the ionic current signal — guiding pore design and detection-condition optimization, and providing theoretical grounding for disease-related molecular recognition and drug-mechanism studies.
